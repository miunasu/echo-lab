Introduction to Text Chunking for RAG Systems

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) combines a parametric language model with a non-parametric memory, typically a vector database of document chunks. Before embedding, long documents must be split into smaller units that fit model context windows and preserve semantic coherence.

Fixed-size chunking is simple and predictable: the text is cut every N characters or tokens, optionally with an overlap so that information near boundaries is not lost. Soft boundary detection can further improve quality by preferring paragraph or sentence breaks near the cut point.

Paragraph-based chunking packs complete paragraphs until the size budget is reached. This strategy works well for well-structured articles, technical reports, and documentation where paragraphs already represent topical units. Oversized paragraphs can be recursively split with a fixed strategy.

Sentence-based chunking is finer-grained. It packs whole sentences into chunks and is especially useful for Q&A corpora and dense retrieval scenarios. Multilingual support matters: Chinese uses 。！？ while English uses .!? and both may include closing quotes or brackets after the terminator.

Sliding-window chunking advances a fixed window with a constant stride. High overlap increases recall at the cost of more chunks and higher storage. In production systems, engineers often tune chunk size between 200 and 800 tokens and overlap between 10% and 20% of the window.

Reliable tooling should emit machine-readable JSON with chunk identifiers, content, character offsets, and overlap metadata. Such output integrates cleanly with distributed pipelines, vector stores, and evaluation harnesses used in modern NLP workflows.
